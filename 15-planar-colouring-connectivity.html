15 Planar Graphs, Euler's Formula, 5- and 6-Colour Theorems, Colouring, Connectivity

1. Why this topic matters

Planarity, colouring, and connectivity are central graph invariants. Euler's formula links vertices, edges, and faces; the 5-colour theorem is a classic induction proof; connectivity measures robustness. These results appear in map colouring, network reliability, and planar graph bounds.

Graph basics, bipartite/Euler, and tree theorems from page 11, page 12, and page 13.

3. Intuition

A planar graph can be drawn without crossings. Euler's formula says for a connected plane graph, V-E+F=2. The picture below is a planar embedding of K4 with 4 faces.

4. Definitions

Planar graph: has a drawing without edge crossings.

Plane graph: a particular planar embedding.

Face: maximal region of the plane not used by the embedding; outer face counts.

Dual: vertices correspond to faces; edges cross original edges.

Chromatic number χ(G): minimum proper vertex colours.

Colour-critical graph: χ(G-v) < χ(G) for every vertex v.

Vertex connectivity κ(G): minimum vertices whose removal disconnects G.

Edge connectivity κ'(G): minimum edges whose removal disconnects G.

5. Theorems and complete proofs

Theorem: Euler's formula

If a connected plane graph has n vertices, e edges, and f faces, then n-e+f=2. Exam likely.

Proof. Induct on n. For n=1, e=0 and f=1, so 1-0+1=2. For n>1, contract a non-loop edge: this reduces n and e by 1 and keeps f unchanged, so induction gives (n-1)-(e-1)+f=2, hence n-e+f=2. ∎

Theorem: planar edge bound

Every simple planar graph with n≥3 vertices has at most 3n-6 edges.

Proof. In a simple planar graph with n≥3, every face boundary has length at least 3, so 2e≥3f. Euler gives f=2-n+e. Substitute: 2e≥3(2-n+e)=6-3n+3e, so e≤3n-6. ∎

Theorem: 6-colour theorem

Every planar graph is 6-colourable.

Proof. By induction on n. A planar graph has a vertex of degree at most 5. Remove it, colour the smaller graph by 6 colours, then the removed vertex has at most 5 coloured neighbours, leaving a free colour. ∎

Theorem: 5-colour theorem

Every planar graph is 5-colourable. Exam likely.

Proof. Induct on n. For n≤5 trivial. For n>5, take a vertex v of degree at most 5. Colour G-v by 5 colours. If neighbours of v use at most 4 colours, colour v with the missing colour. Otherwise v has 5 neighbours v_1,...,v_5 in cyclic order, with distinct colours 1,...,5. Consider the subgraph using colours 1 and 3. If v_1 and v_3 are in different components, swap colours 1 and 3 in v_1's component, freeing colour 1 for v. Otherwise there is a path from v_1 to v_3 using only colours 1 and 3; together with v this forms a cycle separating v_2 from v_4,v_5. Then the subgraph using colours 2 and 4 has v_2 and v_4 in different components; swap to free colour 2. ∎

Theorem: connectivity inequality (not in slides - standard result)

For a simple graph G, κ(G) ≤ κ'(G) ≤ δ(G). Exam likely (Q45).

Proof. κ'≤δ by removing all edges incident to a minimum-degree vertex. For κ≤κ', take a minimum edge cut F; choose one endpoint of each edge in F on one side of the cut. These vertices form a vertex cut of size at most |F|=κ'. ∎

Theorem: strict inequality example

In K_{2,3}, δ=2, κ'=2, κ=2? Actually for a cycle C_n, κ=κ'=2=δ. For a graph where strict inequality holds: take two K4's sharing a single vertex (a "figure-eight"): minimum degree 3, vertex connectivity 1, edge connectivity 3. Removing the shared vertex disconnects; removing three edges incident to the shared vertex in one K4 disconnects. Thus κ=1 < κ'=3 = δ=3. Exam likely (Q45).

Outerplanar graphs and duals

Outerplanar: all vertices on the boundary of the unbounded face. Dual: vertices are faces; face lengths are degrees in the dual; sum of face lengths = 2e.

9. Common mistakes and exam traps

  • Forgetting the outer face in Euler's formula.
  • Using e≤3n-6 for n<3.
  • Confusing 5-colour and 4-colour theorems.
  • Forgetting colour-critical means removing any vertex lowers chromatic number.
  • Confusing vertex and edge connectivity.
Slide errors / not in slides: Vertex/edge connectivity κ, κ' (Q45) and colour-critical graphs (Q46) are not in the slides; marked "Not in slides - standard result". Quiz statement e≥3n-6 is false; correct is e≤3n-6.
