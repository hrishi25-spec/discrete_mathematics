12 Bipartite Graphs, Eulerian Circuits, Degrees, Digraphs, Tournaments, Degree Sequences

1. Why this topic matters

This topic collects several high-value graph theorems: bipartiteness via odd cycles, Euler circuits, digraphs, tournaments and kings, and graphical degree sequences. The proofs are short but precise, and Havel-Hakimi is a standard algorithmic question.

2. Prerequisites

Graph basics, walks, paths, components from page 11.

3. Intuition

A bipartite graph has no odd cycles because going around an odd cycle would force a vertex to have both colors. An Euler circuit exists when every vertex has even degree: you can enter and leave each vertex equally often. The picture below is a 4-cycle, bipartite.

4. Definitions

Bipartite: V can be partitioned into two independent sets.

Euler circuit: closed trail using every edge.

Digraph: directed graph with tails/heads.

Tournament: orientation of complete graph.

King: vertex from which every vertex is reachable by path of length at most 2.

Graphical sequence: nonnegative integers that are degrees of some graph.

5. Theorems and complete proofs

Theorem: cut-edge characterization

An edge is a cut-edge iff it lies in no cycle.

Proof. If e is a cut-edge, removing it disconnects, so no cycle can contain it. Conversely if e lies in a cycle, the cycle provides an alternative path, so removal does not disconnect. ∎

Lemma: closed odd walk contains odd cycle

Every closed odd walk contains an odd cycle.

Proof. Induct on length; if a vertex repeats, delete the closed subwalk and preserve parity/oddness. ∎

Theorem: bipartite iff no odd cycles

A graph is bipartite iff it has no odd cycle. Exam likely (Q34).

Proof. If bipartite, any cycle alternates parts, so length is even. Conversely, fix a vertex in each component, color by parity of path length; no odd cycle ensures well-defined coloring. ∎

Theorem: Euler circuit

A graph is Eulerian iff it has at most one nontrivial component and all vertices have even degree. Exam likely.

Proof. Necessity: each passage through a vertex uses one incoming and one outgoing edge. Sufficiency: induct on edges; an even connected graph has a cycle; remove it; remaining even components have Euler circuits; splice them back. ∎

Theorem: Euler circuit in digraph

A digraph is Eulerian iff d^+(v)=d^-(v) for every v and underlying graph has at most one nontrivial component.

Proof. Necessity is the enter/leave argument. Sufficiency follows as in the undirected case: an Eulerian digraph has a directed cycle; remove it; remaining components have balanced degrees; splice. ∎

Theorem: every tournament has a king

Every tournament has a vertex from which every other vertex is reachable by a path of length at most 2. Exam likely (Q37).

Proof. Take a vertex x of maximum outdegree. For any z not reachable from x in one step, z beats x. If some y is beaten by x and beats z, then x reaches z in two steps. If no such y, then all out-neighbors of x lose to z; counting wins gives contradiction to maximality. ∎

Theorem: Havel-Hakimi

A nonincreasing sequence d_1≥...≥d_n is graphical iff d_2-1,...,d_{d_1+1}-1,d_{d_1+2},...,d_n is graphical (after sorting). Exam likely (Q42).

Proof. If graphical, take a vertex v of degree d_1. Its neighbors can be chosen among the d_1 highest-degree vertices by an exchange argument: if a neighbor has lower degree than a non-neighbor, swapping does not decrease feasibility. Remove v and decrement those degrees to obtain the reduced sequence. Conversely, if the reduced sequence is graphical, add a new vertex adjacent to the first d_1 vertices. ∎

6. Worked examples

Example 1

K_{3,3} is bipartite and 3-regular; its degree sequence 3,3,3,3,3,3 is graphical.

Example 2

Sequence 3,3,2,2,1,1 is graphical? Havel-Hakimi reduces to 2,1,1,1,1 then 0,0,0,0, so yes.

Example 3

Sequence 8,7,6,6,5,3,2,2,2,1 is not graphical (Havel-Hakimi fails). Python verified.

Example 4

A tournament on 3 vertices always has a 3-cycle or transitive ordering; a king exists (the source in transitive case).

Example 5

An even graph decomposes into cycles; this is used in Euler proof.

9. Common mistakes and exam traps

  • Forgetting to sort the reduced degree sequence in Havel-Hakimi.
  • Assuming even degree sum implies graphical (Havel-Hakimi needed).
  • Confusing Euler circuit (closed) with Euler trail.
  • Forgetting connectivity condition for Euler circuits.
  • Using "no odd cycles" but forgetting to check all components.

11. Extra self-test questions

1. Is 3,3,2,2,1,1 graphical?

Yes.

2. Euler circuit condition?

Connected (one nontrivial component) + all degrees even.

3. Digraph Euler condition?

In-degree equals out-degree for every vertex.

4. Is K_{2,3} Eulerian?

No; degrees are 3 and 2.

5. Tournament on 4 vertices always has a king?

Yes.

12. Cheat sheet

Bipartite iff no odd cycles.

Euler circuit: connected + all degrees even.

Digraph Euler: d^+=d^- and connected underlying.

Tournament always has a king.

Havel-Hakimi: reduce by largest degree.

Graphical ⇒ even degree sum (necessary, not sufficient).
