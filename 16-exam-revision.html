Exam Revision

1. Why this page matters

This page collects the formulas and proof ideas from the whole course in one place. It is a revision aid, not a new topic: every result here was proved on an earlier page, and every numerical example has been checked. Use the formula sheets to recall a result, the proof list to reconstruct its idea, and the examples and self-tests to check your speed.

2. Prerequisites

All fifteen topic pages: sets and counting (01, 02), counting refinements (03–09), logic and induction (10), and graph theory (11–15).

3. Intuition

The course is one connected story: counting produces identities, recurrences organise sequences, generating functions turn them into algebra, and graph theory applies the same tools to structures. The map below shows the main routes; each arrow points to a page where the connection is made explicit.

4. Formula sheet: Counting

Subsets: 2^n. Permutations: n!. Ordered selections: n!/(n-r)!.

Combinations: C(n,r). Redundant permutations: n^r. Redundant combinations: C(n+r-1,r).

Circular arrangements: (n-1)!. Surjections: Sur(n,k)=Σ (-1)^i C(k,i)(k-i)^n=k!S(n,k).

Derangements: D_n=n!Σ (-1)^i/i!. Stirling: S(n,k)=kS(n-1,k)+S(n-1,k-1).

Partitions into at most k parts: p_k(n). Bell number B_n=Σ S(n,k).

5. Formula sheet: Recurrences, Generating Functions, Partitions

Homogeneous distinct roots: α_1 r_1^n+α_2 r_2^n. Repeated root: (α_1+α_2 n)r_0^n.

Non-homogeneous: homogeneous + particular; resonance multiply by n^m.

Fibonacci: F_n=Binet formula; G(x)=x/(1-x-x^2).

Compositions into r parts: C(n-1,r-1); GF x^r/(1-x)^r.

Redundant combinations GF: 1/(1-x)^n; coeff C(n+r-1,r).

Partitions: P(n,k)=P(n-1,k-1)+P(n-k,k); p_o=p_d; self-conjugate=distinct odd.

Catalan: C_n=C(2n,n)-C(2n,n-1)=C(2n,n)/(n+1).

6. Formula sheet: Logic and Proofs

P⇒Q false only P true, Q false; contrapositive ¬Q⇒¬P equivalent.

De Morgan: ¬∀xP(x)≡∃x¬P(x); ¬∃xP(x)≡∀x¬P(x).

PMI: base P(1), step P(k)⇒P(k+1). Strong induction uses all 1≤t<k.

Proof methods: direct, contrapositive, contradiction, counterexample, cases, induction.

7. Formula sheet: Graph Theory

Handshaking: sum degrees=2|E|; odd-degree vertices even.

Components ≥ n-m; connected ⇒ m≥n-1. Min degree ≥(n-1)/2 ⇒ connected.

Graph or complement connected. Bipartite iff no odd cycles.

Euler circuit: connected + all degrees even. Digraph: d^+=d^-.

Trees: n-1 edges; labeled trees n^{n-2}; Prüfer length n-2.

MST: Kruskal/Prim correct. Dijkstra: nonnegative weights.

Hall: |N(S)|≥|S| for all S⊆X. Gale-Shapley stable.

Euler plane: n-e+f=2; planar simple e≤3n-6.

Connectivity: κ≤κ'≤δ.

  1. Equivalence relation ↔ partition: classes partition A and vice versa.
  2. Congruence mod n: divisibility gives reflexivity/symmetry/transitivity.
  3. Inclusion-exclusion: element in t sets counted 1 by alternating binomial sum.
  4. Surjections: IE over missing values.
  5. Derangements: IE over fixed points.
  6. Euler totient: IE over prime divisors.

  8. Odd = distinct partitions: generating-function identity.
  9. Self-conjugate = distinct odd: fold Ferrers diagram.
  10. Catalan: reflection principle bijection.

10. Common mistakes and exam traps

  1. Subsets versus arrangements. Wrong: 5 elements give 5!=120 subsets. Right: each element is in or out, so 25=32; 120 is the number of orders.
  2. Combinations versus repetition. Wrong: choosing 2 of 4 objects with repetition gives C(4,2)=6. Right: repetition allowed gives C(4+2−1,2)=C(5,2)=10; 6 is the no-repetition count.
  3. Derangement index and probability. Wrong: D4=44 and P(no fixed point)=1/e. Right: D4=9, so the probability is 9/24=3/8; D5=44 and 1/e is only the large-n limit.
  4. Catalan off by one. Wrong: parenthesizations of 4 factors = C4=14. Right: n+1 factors give Cn, so 4 factors give C3=5.
  5. Sign in Euler's formula. Wrong: a connected plane graph satisfies n+e+f=2. Right: n−e+f=2; for K4, 4−6+4=2.
  6. Wrong planarity bound. Wrong: K3,3 satisfies 9≤3·6−6=12 so the bound proves planarity. Right: it is bipartite, so use e≤2n−4=8, and 9>8 shows non-planarity.
  7. Hall checked only on singletons. Wrong: every singleton has a neighbour, so three jobs and two people can all be filled. Right: the full set has |N|=2<3, so Hall fails.

11. Worked examples

Example 1: surjections

Sur(5,4) = 45−4·35+6·25−4·15 = 1024−972+192−4 = 240, matching 4!·S(5,4)=24·10.

Example 2: derangements

D4 = 4!(1−1+1/2−1/6+1/24) = 24−24+12−4+1 = 9, so the no-fixed-point probability is 9/24 = 3/8.

Example 3: Catalan

C3 = C(6,3)−C(6,2) = 20−15 = 5, the number of parenthesizations of four factors.

Example 4: Euler's formula

A plane embedding of K4 has n=4, e=6 and f=4 (including the outer face), so 4−6+4=2.

Example 5: labeled trees

Prüfer codes of length n−2 give 64 = 1296 labeled trees on 6 vertices.

Example 6: a Stirling number

S(5,3) = 3S(4,3)+S(4,2) = 3·6+7 = 25, also (35−3·25+3)/3! = 150/6.

Example 7: a matching

In the path P4, edges 1-2 and 3-4 form a matching of size 2; edges 1-2 and 2-3 share vertex 2 and cannot both be used.

12. Extra self-test questions

1. How many subsets has a 4-element set?

Each element is independently in or out, so 24=16.

2. In how many orders can 10 objects be arranged 4 at a time?

P(10,4)=10·9·8·7=5040, equivalently 10!/6!.

3. How many 4-subsets has a 10-set?

C(10,4)=(10·9·8·7)/(4·3·2·1)=5040/24=210.

4. How many surjections from a 5-set to a 4-set?

Sur(5,4)=45−4·35+6·25−4·15=240.

5. What is D5?

D5=5!(1−1+1/2−1/6+1/24−1/120)=120−120+60−20+5−1=44.

6. What is the third Catalan number?

C3=C(6,3)−C(6,2)=20−15=5.

7. How many edges has a tree on 10 vertices?

A tree on n vertices has n−1 edges, so 9.

8. Is K5 planar?

No. It has 10 edges, but a simple planar graph on 5 vertices has at most 3·5−6=9.
