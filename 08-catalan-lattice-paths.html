08 Catalan Numbers and Lattice Paths

1. Why this topic matters

Catalan numbers count parenthesizations, triangulations, Dyck paths, and many other recursively decomposable structures. The reflection principle is the key technique for counting paths that stay on one side of a diagonal. Exam questions usually ask for Catalan C_n or for a reflection-principle count.

2. Prerequisites

Basic counting and combinations from page 02.

3. Intuition

Consider monotone paths from (0,0) to (n,n) using steps east and north. Total paths are C(2n,n). "Good" paths never rise above the diagonal y=x. The first bad path touches the line y=x+1; reflect the rest of the path across that line to get a path to (n-1,n+1). This bijection counts bad paths as C(2n,n-1). The picture shows a good path and a reflected bad path.

4. Definitions

Monotone lattice path: path from (0,0) to (a,b) using east and north steps.

Catalan number: C_n = (1/(n+1)) C(2n,n).

Bad path: path from (0,0) to (n,n) that at some point has y > x (goes above diagonal).

Reflection principle: reflect part of a path after first crossing a boundary to count bad paths.

5. Theorems and complete proofs

Theorem: number of monotone paths

Paths from (0,0) to (a,b) = C(a+b,a). Exam likely.

Proof. A path has a+b steps, of which a are east; choose their positions. ∎

Theorem: Catalan number

C_n = C(2n,n) - C(2n,n-1) = (1/(n+1)) C(2n,n). Exam likely.

Proof. Total paths from (0,0) to (n,n) is C(2n,n). Bad paths first touch y=x+1; reflect the suffix after that touch across y=x+1, giving a bijection to paths from (0,0) to (n-1,n+1), counted by C(2n,n-1). Subtract. Simplify: C(2n,n)-C(2n,n-1)=C(2n,n)/(n+1). ∎

Theorem: reflection principle (general)

Paths from (0,0) to (n,n) that touch or cross y=x+1 are in bijection with paths from (0,0) to (n-1,n+1).

Proof. Reflect the portion after the first touch of y=x+1. ∎

Example 1

C_3 = C(6,3)-C(6,2)=20-15=5.

Example 2

Number of monotone paths from (0,0) to (3,5) = C(8,3)=56.

Example 3

Bad paths for n=6: C(12,5)=792. Good (Catalan) paths: C_6=132.

Example 4

Paths from (0,0) to (6,6) that cross the diagonal exactly once (change sides) = 330 (Python verified).

Example 5

Parenthesizations of 4 factors: C_3=5.

9. Common mistakes and exam traps

  • Confusing "touch diagonal" with "cross diagonal" (change sides).
  • Using C(2n,n) instead of Catalan for good paths.
  • Reflecting across the wrong line (y=x vs y=x+1).
  • Forgetting the 1/(n+1) factor in Catalan.

10. Practice questions (assigned to this page)

Q26. Number of monotone paths from (0,0) to (6,6) crossing the diagonal exactly once.

Solution: changing sides of the diagonal exactly once gives 330. Python verification: 330. (If "touch the diagonal at one interior point" is intended, the count is 168.)

12. Cheat sheet

Total paths (a,b): C(a+b,a).

Catalan: C_n=C(2n,n)-C(2n,n-1)=C(2n,n)/(n+1).

Bad paths: C(2n,n-1).

Reflection across y=x+1.

Parenthesizations of n+1 factors: C_n.
